Everyone has a task that sits untouched for weeks. In running a mosque committee, it might be an activity report that is a month overdue and still unwritten. Every time it comes to mind, it feels heavy, so it gets pushed aside again.

After going through this many times, I realized it is rarely about laziness. The task is too big, it has no fixed time, and we are not ready to start it. Those three problems can be handled one at a time.

## Break It Into Small Steps

A task called "write the activity report" sounds like a mountain. The brain refuses right away. Try splitting it into steps that fit in 15 to 25 minutes, such as gathering photos, writing an outline, then writing one section.

When I can't see how to split it, I ask an AI agent for help. Just describe the task and ask for a list of small steps in order. For mosque matters this helps a lot, because tasks often involve many people and many files. The result is often more detailed than I would have come up with, and the first step suddenly becomes obvious.

## Lock In the Time, Set a Reminder

"I'll do it when I get a chance" almost never comes true. Free time does not show up by itself. We have to make room for it.

Pick one specific hour in your schedule, say Thursday morning after dawn prayer, and treat it like an appointment with another person. So I don't forget, I send a note to my own WhatsApp the moment a task appears, and I set an automatic reminder that fires at that scheduled time. A reminder that arrives on time beats good intentions every time.

## Remove What Makes You Stall

Often we put things off because something is missing. The data is incomplete, a file can't be found, or we don't know where to begin. Before the work hour arrives, gather everything you need so that you can simply start when you sit down.

Then begin with the easiest or most enjoyable part. That is a gentle way in. Once your hands are moving, the hard part feels closer.

One more rule: finish one thing completely before moving to another. Opening five tasks at once leaves everything half done, and the relief never arrives.

## The Short Version

These three ways work well together.

- Split the task into 15 to 25 minute steps.
- Lock in a specific hour and set an automatic reminder.
- Prepare your materials, start with the easiest part, finish one thing at a time.

This week, pick the task that has waited longest. Split it, schedule it, prepare for it, and do the first step. Usually after 20 minutes, the weight that felt huge has already shrunk a lot.

I still get tempted to put things off myself. The difference is that now I have a way back on track without blaming myself.
