We hear a lot about AI agents making work faster. But when you ask how many hours were saved or how much costs dropped, many teams can only give vague answers. Without clear numbers, it is hard to decide whether the investment is worth keeping.

At Masjid AL-HIKMAH, we run an AI agent called Utsman to help with administration and communication. Utsman runs on a large language model, connects through WhatsApp and Telegram channels, and has scheduled automation for sending routine reminders. At first, we also did not know what to measure. Over time, we found the metrics that fit our workflow.

The easiest one to track is time saved. Before Utsman, answering community questions via chat took one to two hours each day. After the agent went live, many common questions were answered automatically. We logged it: we now save about 60 to 90 minutes on average every day. This number is real and directly felt by the team.

The second metric is tasks completed without human intervention. Examples include study schedule reminders, attendance confirmations, or location directions. Where everything used to be handled manually, most of it now runs on its own. We call this the "self-completion rate." The higher the number, the greater the agent's benefit.

Third, do not forget quality. Speed means nothing if the answers are wrong or confuse people. We always set aside time for occasional review, reading conversations that need correction, and updating the agent's instructions. If quality drops, that is a signal the agent needs tuning.

There is another factor that often gets overlooked: return usage. Are community members and administrators still using the AI service after the first month? If usage frequency drops, something is off. Maybe the answers are not accurate enough, or maybe access is not easy enough. This data helps us improve the system regularly.

Research from various technology companies shows that organizations successfully measuring AI impact always start with a baseline. They first record how long a process takes before the agent steps in. Then they compare it with the condition afterward. Without a baseline, you are only guessing.

In practice, you do not need complicated metrics. Just pick two or three processes that happen often, measure their time and cost, then monitor changes over three months. The results will give a clear picture of whether the AI agent truly helps or is just extra spending.

Most importantly, measurement is not just about numbers. It is about new working habits. When the admin team has more time, they can focus on more strategic tasks. Relations with the community improve because responses are faster. And most valuably, the organization becomes more open to technology innovation without losing its human touch.
