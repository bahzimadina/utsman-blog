An idea shows up in the shower, on the motorbike, or exactly as you are falling asleep. The sentences already feel formed in your head. The next morning only one thing survives: the certainty that there was a good idea, with no memory of what it was.

What disappears is not the idea. It is the gap between it appearing and you writing it down. The longer that gap, the thinner what is left.

## The two-minute gap that kills ideas

Most people postpone it, waiting for the right conditions: sit down, open the laptop, find the right file. Good intention, zero result. By the time all of that is ready, the idea has evaporated.

There is a more honest way. Write it now, in the roughest form. One sentence, three keywords, a ten-second voice recording if your hands are busy, pulled over first if you are on the road.

It does not need to be tidy or complete, and nobody needs an explanation. Tidiness can follow later. Loss cannot be undone.

## One place, not seven

The next problem is usually not forgetting to write, but forgetting where you wrote it. Notes scatter across a notebook, a notes app, a chat with yourself, scraps of paper on the desk.

Pick one place as the first container. Every idea goes there, including the ones that are not clear yet. Other places are fine, as long as they are not the first stop.

Choose what sits closest to your hand. If opening it takes four taps, skip it. Notes can be moved later; a lost idea cannot be called back.

## Do not judge while capturing

This is where many people stop. A fresh idea gets written down and immediately put on trial: too simple, no time, someone already tried it. The result is an empty note and a crowded head.

Separate the two jobs. Capturing is about speed. Judging needs its own time.

- Write first, filter later.
- Add the date, that is enough.
- Do not delete ideas that feel strange; strange ones often turn into good material a month later.

## The tool nearest your hand

Paper notebooks still work, but most people are closer to their phone than to a pen, so the phone wins.

The tidier setups have layers: rough notes land in one app, then get cleaned up once a week. Automatic help earns its keep here. An AI agent can turn messy notes into a summary, give them a title, and group ideas by theme. Notes that arrive through WhatsApp or email can be routed automatically, so nothing is copied by hand.

There are limits. A model can misread context, or merge two ideas that are different. A human still has to read the result.

## Fifteen minutes a week

Open the notes once a week and sort through them. Delete what no longer matters, merge the duplicates, mark one or two that deserve work. Let the rest sleep in the file; not every idea has to become a project.

The habit is small, but your head feels lighter once it stops being a warehouse.

The last idea whose content you lost, where do you think it showed up?
