Routine work often stalls, and not because the people doing it lack skill. The reason is plainer than that: nobody wrote down how it gets done. It lives in the head of one or two people, and when they are away, everyone else is unsure where to begin.

I handle administration for a mosque committee, and I have run into this myself many times. The monthly meeting still happens, but the invitations go out late. The activity recap exists, but its format changes with every board member. Nobody is at fault. The method was simply never written down.

## What a simple SOP is

It is not a thick document of dozens of pages. It is one page that answers four questions: who does the work, when it is done, what the steps are, and what to do when something goes wrong.

If that page can be read in two minutes, the size is right.

## How to make one

Pick just one routine activity, the one that repeats most often. Think of the monthly meeting, the activity recap, or incoming mail. Don't try three at once, or none will get finished.

Then write it from the experience of the person who normally does it, not from theory. The best way is to ask that person directly. Have them walk you through it from start to finish, and note it as it is, including the small habits nobody ever mentions.

The minimum ingredients for one page:

- A purpose in a sentence or two, then 5–7 steps in order
- The role responsible at each step
- What must be ready before starting, and what must exist once it is done

## What people miss

The most common mistake is writing an SOP like a rulebook for blaming people. The sentences are full of prohibitions, as if it were prepared to find out who slipped.

A good SOP makes work lighter. It does not add bureaucracy. If the job feels heavier after you wrote it down, the SOP needs fixing, not the person. The "if something goes wrong" part should hold a way out, not a punishment.

## Why it must be in everyday language

The goal is for a new member to follow it right away without asking anyone. If the wording is stiff and full of jargon, people will still go find a senior to ask, and the SOP turns into decoration.

Write as if you were explaining to a friend who just joined. Short sentences, clear verbs, no jumping around in the order.

An AI assistant or digital notes can help tidy the structure and wording of a draft. But the content still has to come from the person who does the work, and a human stays in charge of the SOP.

## Start small

Pick one routine activity today. Write it up in 30 minutes, and don't aim for perfect. Then ask someone else to follow the page, and watch where they get stuck.

The spot where they stumble is the part to fix. Once that is done, move on to the next activity.
