Last week I opened my planning notebook again. Twenty lines, and almost none of them had moved. Not because of laziness, but because the list was simply too long to chase alone while still handling the daily schedule.

So for next week I cut it down to three. Not the easiest three, but the three that will make the rest of the work lighter once they are done.

## One: the activity schedule and its reminders

Prayer group sessions, committee meetings, and the cleaning rota are still written on a whiteboard and scattered across several different chat groups. The result is predictable. Someone always forgets, and we only find out once the event has already started.

Next week I want a single door. Every schedule goes into one place, and reminders go out automatically through the channels people already use, which are WhatsApp and Telegram. The agent that runs on a schedule is already in place. It just needs tidying so messages do not arrive twice and the timing does not drift.

## Two: the paperwork that is still scattered

Incoming letters, attendance sheets, and activity notes currently live in each person's private folder. If someone needs a file, they have to ask around first to find out who kept it. It is small work, but left alone it turns into a big problem.

The target for next week is simple. File names and dates follow one pattern, everything sits in a shared folder, and one person knows what is inside. No expensive new app needed. What it takes is an agreed naming rule and the habit of saving things in the same place.

## Three: a tidier workflow for the agent

I now use the AI agent for a lot of things: summarising documents, drafting messages, arranging schedules, and reminding me about deadlines. The language model and the MCP layer behind it run steadily enough.

What is missing is the record. Which tasks have run, which are waiting, and who checks the output. Next week I want to write a single page describing the workflow: what the task is, which channel it uses, and where the result is stored. That way the automation does not run on its own without anyone looking at it.

## Why only three?

Because three things actually finished are far more useful than ten that are half done. A long list usually just moves from the notebook into your head, then quietly turns into background weight.

## Quick questions

**What if one of them does not get finished?** Move it to the following week without guilt, as long as you do not add new items to the same list.

**Why not adopt a new app at the same time?** Because the problem is rarely the tool, it is the habit. A new tool without rules just gives you another place to keep the mess.

**Who does the work?** I start the first two myself and ask one person to help with the notes. The automation side I do in small steps, one workflow per day, so it is easy to check.

Next week may not be perfect. At least those three things will no longer be just shapes in my head, and I will know clearly which ones are done. 🐘
