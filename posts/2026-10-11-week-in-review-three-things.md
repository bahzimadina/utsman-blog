This week felt packed. Work matters, an organisation event, and a few jobs I had wanted to finish for a long while. Looking back, though, what helped most was not a new tool. It was the small habits I repeated every day.

Here are three things that paid off this week. Maybe one of them fits your routine too.

## Notes you can actually see

Early in the week I wrote a single page of notes with the commands and steps I use most. Ten lines, kept somewhere easy to spot. The effect was plain: I stopped hunting for a way of doing something I had already figured out before.

A page like that looks trivial, yet it quiets the mind. Nothing has to be memorised. If I forget, I look.

## Clearing what has been hanging

The second thing that helped: picking three small jobs that had been sitting around, then finishing them one at a time. Not the biggest ones, the smallest. Reply to one message, tidy one folder, close one matter that needed a single step.

Mental weight often comes from a pile of small unfinished things, not from one large task. Once those three were done, the rest of the week sat lighter.

## Help from an assistant

I use an AI agent for some routine work. Parts of it run on a schedule, such as a morning summary and regular reminders. Other parts I call when needed, through WhatsApp or Telegram, sometimes by email when the matter is longer.

What makes this useful is not a clever model. It is a clear instruction. When I write the request in detail — what to do, where the data comes from, what shape the result should take — the answer comes back far tidier. Vague instructions produce vague results.

One rule I hold to: I always check the assistant's work before using it. Especially when numbers or people's names are involved. Verification stays a human job.

## Questions that keep coming up

A few questions come up again and again. Do I need to write code? No. It matters more to explain what you want, calmly and in order.

Do I need many tools? Not necessarily. One chat channel, one place to take notes, and one habit of checking results are enough to start.

On safety, the principle is simple: never put something confidential into the wrong channel, and never let an assistant make decisions unchecked. The rest is habit.

The lesson for me this week: progress does not always arrive with a new tool. Often it arrives with one page of notes, three small jobs finished, and an instruction written clearly.

Next week I want to clear three things still open. What will you start with? 🙌
