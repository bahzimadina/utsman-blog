Since AI assistants started helping with daily work, the questions in my inbox tend to repeat. Some are curious, some worry about data leaking, some are disappointed when an answer misses. These are the answers I usually give in a WhatsApp group or over coffee. 🤖

## What an AI assistant really is

A large language model like GPT, Claude, or Gemini runs on one basic task: guessing the next word. From billions of text samples it learns patterns, then builds sentences that sound reasonable. That is why it can draft a letter, summarise notes, or sketch a schedule, though it never knows what is in our heads.

It does not open a database of answers. It improvises the most likely one. That is both the strength and the weakness.

## Why answers miss sometimes

The phenomenon is called hallucination. A model can name a committee member who does not exist, invent the wrong meeting date, or quote a verse that sounds close but is off. Its tone stays confident, so the pull to trust it straight away is strong.

My habit is simple: treat the first output as a draft. Numbers, dates, names, and quotations get checked against the original source. For admin work that touches residents or volunteer schedules, manual correction is not optional.

## Where the data goes

It comes back to the provider. Conversations are generally sent to the model's servers, and as far as I know some providers train on customer data unless that option is switched off in settings. So no identity numbers, bank account numbers, login details, or private chats go into the box.

The safer route: rewrite the data in general form. Just "a resident", not a full name. When something truly needs privacy, a model running locally is an option, though it asks more technical work.

## Why context changes the result

The model does not know our organisation's habits. Not the report format we use, who gets a copy, or when meetings usually start. All of that has to be spelled out.

Compare two requests. "Write a report on the activity" gives a generic skeleton. "Write a report on the monthly study circle for October, A4, sections: attendance, material, evaluation, next steps, formal tone, one page max" comes back much closer to usable.

## Chat versus agent

A plain chat only replies. An agent can act: read files, run scheduled jobs, send messages through WhatsApp or Telegram, draft email, and connect to other apps through a protocol such as MCP. Less a typist, more a staff member who can carry out small steps.

The limit stays with the human. Posting to a residents' group, publishing to social media, or changing records should wait for approval. I prefer an agent that drafts and a person who presses send.

## Small habits that help

- Name the final shape you want: table, paragraphs, or short points.
- Show one example of output you liked before.
- Ask it to flag the parts it is unsure about.
- Split a big job into several short requests.

These four habits sound trivial, and they change the output a lot. Time that used to go into repeating instructions moves to work that needs a human brain.

An AI assistant is like a new staff member who reads and writes fast but has no idea how the organisation ticks. It earns its place not by being always right, but by cutting the rough work: summarising, drafting, sorting, reminding. The judgement and the responsibility stay with the person giving directions.
