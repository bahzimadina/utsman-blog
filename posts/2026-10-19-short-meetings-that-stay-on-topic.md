Ever sat in a meeting scheduled for 30 minutes, glanced at the clock, and found that 90 minutes had gone by? I have, many times. On a mosque committee, meetings that overrun are almost a tradition.

Over time I realized it was never about people lacking discipline. It was about preparation. No agenda, nobody watching the clock, so the conversation drifts wherever it likes. Everyone goes home tired and late, and the decisions are still hanging.

## Prepare before the invitation goes out

Write the agenda first, then send the invitation. Every item gets three things: a purpose, an owner, and a time limit. For example, "set the date for the community clean-up, owned by the general affairs team, ten minutes".

Send the agenda to the WhatsApp or Telegram group a day ahead. People then arrive with their material in hand instead of starting to think from zero in the room.

Start on time, even if not everyone is there. Count who is present and begin. Latecomers catch up without the group repeating the whole discussion. After two or three rounds, people start arriving early on their own.

## Two small roles

Appoint a timekeeper and a note-taker. These are not official positions, just tasks for one meeting. Next week someone else takes a turn. The timekeeper only needs to say, "two minutes left on this item." The note-taker writes down decisions, not every word spoken.

## When the talk wanders

Someone will always bring up something else. That is normal. No need for a sharp reprimand, just ask: "which item number does this belong to?"

If it has no number, write it on a parking list and go back to the agenda. The parking list is handled at the end if time remains. The person whose idea was noted usually relaxes, because they feel heard.

## Closing and following up

Close the meeting with three things:

- what was decided
- who will do it
- when it will be done

A meeting without all three is just a long chat.

Send the meeting notes the same day, short, one page at most. This is what stops the same meeting from happening again next week, because everyone holds the same record.

## A little help from technology

An AI agent can help with the dull parts: drafting an agenda from the previous meeting's notes, summarizing the minutes, and sending reminders through WhatsApp, Telegram, or email on a schedule.

But decisions stay with people. The draft is read and corrected first, and the person responsible presses send.

## One last thing

A short meeting is not a rushed meeting. Precisely because time is limited, people arrive better prepared and the decisions come out clearer. Try it once this week and see the difference.
