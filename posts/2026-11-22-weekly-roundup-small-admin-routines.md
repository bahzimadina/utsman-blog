No big project wrapped up this week. What happened was the opposite: a few small routines repeated every day, and the effect showed up by the end of the week. The inbox is tidier, no meetings collided, and two reports landed before their deadlines. So this roundup is about those unglamorous habits, not about big wins.

## Twenty minutes before starting work

The first twenty minutes of each morning go to clearing whatever was left from yesterday. Open the notes, reread messages nobody answered yet, flag what has to be done today. There is no heavy work in that session. Its only job is making sure nothing small quietly slips away. Since I started doing it consistently, I rarely find a message that sat unanswered for days.

## One place for every note

Notes used to scatter: some in a notebook, some in a chat app, the rest stacked up in my head. Now everything goes into a single note file. One heading per matter, the date up front, done or not done marked clearly. When someone asks about something, I just search the heading. The time spent writing a note is a few seconds; the time saved looking for it later is far bigger.

## Templates for repeated messages

There are about five messages I send almost every week: meeting invitations, schedule confirmations, data requests, deadline reminders, and thank-yous after an event. All of them now have a ready version, and I only adjust names and dates. Writing takes less time, and the tone stays polite because it was thought through once, calmly.

## Automation that stays out of the way

Recurring calendar reminders handle anything on a fixed schedule. For the shifting stuff, a bot in the team's chat channel does enough: a short command, and the result appears in the same group. I also started using an AI agent built on a language model to draft rough announcements. I always read and edit the output before it goes anywhere, never send it raw. The time saving is not about sophistication; it is that the first draft no longer starts from a blank page.

## Ten minutes to close the week

Friday afternoon I stop for a moment. Three lines only: what got finished, what got pushed, what needs preparing for Monday. Those notes become the material for a roundup like this one. Simple, but it stops Monday from feeling like starting at zero.

## Quick questions

Only room for one routine — which one? The twenty morning minutes. It shows results fastest and it is the easiest to keep.

Do I need special software? No. The notes app that ships with the phone plus a calendar is enough. Fancier tools often become the reason to postpone.

What if the week falls apart and the routine breaks? Start again tomorrow morning, don't wait for Monday. A small routine does not collapse from one missed day, but it does collapse if you leave it for weeks.

That was the week. Nothing special, and that is the point: things you can repeat without much effort are usually the ones that last 🗂
