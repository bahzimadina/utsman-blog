Open your meeting notes from last month and read them as a stranger would. Chances are they say something like: "follow-up -> B, check again, not fixed, waiting." You understand it, because the context lives in your head. Everyone else sees a riddle.

Personal notes were never meant for other people. The trouble starts when we want to share them as a report, a summary, or a blog post, and the shape is still the one we scribbled in a hurry.

## What raw notes are missing

Three things usually go missing. First, a reason for the reader to care. Second, a clear order from start to finish. Third, concrete detail that replaces your private code: the abbreviations, arrows, and sentence fragments.

None of these appear on their own. You have to add them on purpose, and that is a different job from taking notes.

## How to tidy them up

I usually start with the most boring step: pasting all the notes into a single file. No cleanup yet. The point is to have everything in one place so you can see the pattern.

Then pick one imagined reader. Not "everyone", but one clear person, say a colleague who missed that meeting. The question "what does she need to know?" will cut half your notes without any effort.

Next, rewrite the first sentence as the conclusion. Don't leave the point of the piece for the last paragraph. A busy reader may stop at sentence two, so the opening has to say what happened and why it matters.

After that, remove whatever only matters to you. Private reminders, doubts that are already settled, and dead ends that changed nothing can go. Finally, replace abbreviations with full sentences. "Not fixed" becomes "the fix isn't finished, and we're waiting for confirmation from another team."

## Help from an AI agent

My habit is to let notes from meetings and WhatsApp messages pile up for a few days. Then I ask an AI agent to help tidy them: put them in order, suggest an opening line, and flag the parts where the context is unclear. The result is not a finished piece. It is a draft that I still reread and reshape until it sounds like me.

One rule I keep: I only give the agent material that is safe to share. It is quick at fixing form. Deciding what deserves to be opened up to other people stays a human job.

## The short version

If you want it fast, hold on to these five steps:

- Paste all your notes into one file.
- Pick one reader.
- Make the first sentence the conclusion.
- Cut what only helps you.
- Swap abbreviations for full sentences.

Nothing complicated there. The hard part is building the habit of pausing before you hit send and asking: if someone opens this without me beside them, will they get it?

If the answer is not yet, your notes aren't done becoming writing. That is fine. One more pass is usually enough.
