After a mosque event or study session, our phones are usually full of photos and videos. On the laptop, meeting notes and correspondence files are scattered across the desktop. Two weeks later, when asked for a report, we panic because we forgot where the files were stored. 😅

This happens in many organizations, including mosques. A tidy digital archive helps a lot with reporting, transparency, and future event evaluation. Without a clear storage system, hours are wasted searching for files that should be available in seconds.

## Practical Steps from the Field

The most important first step is immediate sorting. Within one or two days after the event ends, spend ten to fifteen minutes cleaning up files. Delete blurry photos, duplicates, or unnecessary videos. Do not let raw files pile up for too long because they become harder to clean later.

Second, use a folder structure based on year and month. For example: `2026/10-Muharram/Kajian-Syariah/`. Inside it, create subfolders `Photos`, `Videos`, and `Documents`. This way, anyone searching for files does not need to open dozens of random folders.

Third, give files descriptive and consistent names. Avoid names like `IMG_1234.jpg` or `document1.pdf`. Instead use a date and event name pattern, such as `2026-10-17_Kajian-Syariah_Documentation.jpg`. Clear names make searching easier even without opening each file.

Fourth, separate visual documentation from administrative documents like meeting notes, permits, or attendance lists. Administrative files are usually accessed more often for reporting or licensing purposes.

Fifth, always keep backups in at least two different places. Save a copy on the organization's laptop and upload to cloud storage like Google Drive. If a device breaks or data is lost, the backup becomes the main rescue.

Lastly, get used to writing down the archive storage location in meeting notes or an agenda book. This small note is very useful for the next committee members taking over similar tasks, making work transitions smoother.

With these simple habits, event documentation becomes far more structured. No paid software or special technical skills are needed. What is required is commitment and consistency from the whole team.
